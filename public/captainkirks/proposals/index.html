Proposals

They can't see it coming. The crew can.

A private boat is one of the only proposal spots left with no crowd, no strangers, and no chance of someone walking through the shot. Just the two of you, a discreet crew, and the Golden Gate behind you.

How it usually goes

One phone call. The secret stays with us.

  • Call us directly. We've staged this more times than we can count, and the plan stays between us.
  • The crew knows the moment is coming. They stay out of frame and let it happen.
  • Most couples pick one of three spots: under the Golden Gate, off Sausalito at golden hour, or in the lee of Angel Island, where the water goes calm.
  • Bring your own champagne, no corkage fee. A photographer counts as a guest.

The boat

Mahalo. Built for two.

Mahalo is 35 feet, six guests max. Small enough that it never stops feeling private. A weekday evening runs $175 an hour with a three-hour minimum, which puts the whole boat at about what you'd pay for a nice dinner for four. Weekends are $200 an hour.

From the Captain's Log

Some days the Bay gives you everything.

In December 2019, a couple came aboard a year to the day after he'd proposed on a bike ride through the Marin Headlands. They sailed past the Golden Gate and tried the howl that gives you echoes under the span: it takes volume and timing to catch. Pelicans dove off the bow the whole way back to Sausalito.

And when the wedding comes, we do those too.

The details, handled

  • Sunset timing is handled for you: we cast off about two and a half hours before, so the light hits on schedule.
  • Seasick-prone partner? Tell us. We'll pick the calm route and the right boat for the day.
  • Rain means a free reschedule. No lost deposit, no lost surprise.
