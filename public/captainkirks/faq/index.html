Good to know

Everything people ask before they sail.

We tried to answer it all here — prices, what to wear, kids and dogs, weather, where to park. Anything we missed, call Sue at (650) 930-0740 and she'll sort you out.

Booking & prices

Are your prices per person or for the whole boat?

Whole boat, not per person. Bring anywhere from 1 to 6 guests on Mahalo, or up to 20 on Bay Wolf and Osprey (16 on weekends, 20 for corporate trips), for the same price. It's always a private charter — your group and our crew, no strangers aboard.

How do I book my trip?

Give us a call and we'll help you pick the right trip. We just need your contact info, a card for the deposit, a rough headcount (easy to adjust later), and where you'd like to sail from. You'll get a confirmation letter with all the details.

What if I need to change the day of the trip?

No financial penalty, ever. Give us as much notice as you can, and if you're not sure your group's availability yet, we'll hold the trip indefinitely — your paid amount stays on the books until you're ready for a new date.

What is the cancellation policy?

Flexible and friendly is the goal. Move your date, or take a gift certificate for the full amount, any time it doesn't work for you. If we cancel for weather, you get a reschedule, gift certificate, or full refund — we eat the fees, not you. If you cancel: more than a week out it's 15%, inside a week it's 50%, and inside 24 hours it's 100% unless we can rebook the slot. Our motto really is "Sailing with Friends," and we try to live it.

On the boat

What should we wear?

Dress in layers — long sleeves, a sweatshirt, a wind jacket at minimum — plus long pants and soft-soled, closed-toe shoes (sneakers, not heels or cowboy boots). Bring a camera and sunscreen. On sunset or winter trips go warmer still: a real jacket, gloves, and a hat that won't blow off. Water's always cooler than land once the wind picks up.

May food and drinks be brought onboard?

Absolutely — bring your own or use our caterer, no corkage fee either way. Finger foods and cupcakes travel better than cake. We'll have some cups, plates, and napkins aboard, but for a bigger group, bring your own. Beer, white wine, and champagne are welcome in moderation; no red wine and no hard liquor. We keep it to about one drink per person per hour, for a simple reason: friends don't let friends fall off of sailboats.

Do you have a set itinerary or schedule?

Every trip's a little different — we read the wind and tide and go from there, usually counter-clockwise. A typical four-hour sail takes in the Sausalito and San Francisco waterfronts, the Golden Gate Bridge (and past it, weather permitting), the Presidio, Crissy Field, Fisherman's Wharf, the financial district and Bay Bridge, Oracle Park, a close pass of Alcatraz, and Angel Island. Tell us what you're after and we'll route around it.

Can we stop somewhere during the trip?

Most trips stay underway — there's plenty to see and not many spots to tie up. When we do stop, it's usually Sam's Anchor Café in Tiburon for lunch, or a picnic and barbecue on Angel Island. Stopping tacks on an hour or more, and a few spots charge docking fees or fill up on weekends. We can also run a one-way trip and drop you somewhere for a meal — grab a cab or the bus back to your car after.

Is there any narration provided?

Our captains have sailed this Bay for 20-plus years and know it well — the currents, the wind, the history, the wildlife, which building is which. Ask as many questions as you like, or none at all; they're happy to talk or just sail.

What activities do passengers participate in?

Whatever you're in the mood for. Help trim a sail, take a turn at the helm for as long as you like, snap photos of the wildlife, or just sit back with a drink while the captain calls out the sights. You're welcome to take the helm — nobody's required to.

What do people tend to enjoy most about these cruises?

It's different for everyone — dolphins outside the Gate, a good sunset, steering in real wind, a long-overdue conversation with family, bonding with coworkers. Mostly it's the chance to unplug: no outside distractions, just you, the wind, the water, and the boat. For a lot of guests it's something they've always wanted to do, and they remember it for years.

Is there an enclosed cabin?

Osprey and Mahalo both have small enclosed cabins with a sitting area, though seasickness is rare and, if it happens, it's usually below deck where you lose sight of the horizon. Most people stay up in the cockpit anyway — our canvas dodger keeps it dry and warm, and the views are better. Every boat has a head, a real marine toilet, not a bucket.

Do we have to wear life jackets (PFDs)?

The Coast Guard doesn't require it, but we do — safety's not optional here. We carry comfortable Type II life jackets that won't cramp your sailing.

Kids, dogs & guests

Are children allowed on all tours?

Yes. If your child is under 4, give us a call first so we can plan around them. Kids 5 and up can join in as long as there's at least one adult per child and they can follow safety instructions — our captains genuinely enjoy teaching kids and teens to sail.

Are dogs allowed on the boats?

Dogs are welcome — bring the whole crew. We carry dog life jackets aboard, so your pup's covered too.

Weather & what-ifs

What if the weather is bad?

We sail year-round and most days are great for it. Rain in summer and fall is rare; if it's windy we can duck into calmer water, and if it's foggy, Sausalito's usually clear even when the city isn't. If real winter rain rolls in, we reschedule or refund you in full — if it's raining, you do not have to go sailing.

Getting there

What are the pickup locations?

From Sausalito, every boat in the fleet departs with easy, free parking. From San Francisco, Osprey and Bay Wolf depart from Pier 40, next to Oracle Park in South Beach (a docking fee applies there). We'll email you exact directions once you book.

Where do I park?

In Sausalito, Osprey and Mahalo dock at Clipper Harbor Marina with large free lots, and Bay Wolf ties up nearby with free street parking. At Pier 40 in San Francisco, there are several lots within a few blocks, sometimes street parking, and a short loading zone right at the pier. Captain Kirk's own pick is Impark at 541 The Embarcadero, San Francisco, CA 94105, right at Bryant and the Embarcadero, just north of Pier 40.
